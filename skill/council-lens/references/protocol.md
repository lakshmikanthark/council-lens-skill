# Council protocol

Use this reference when running Standard or Deep Council, or when a Fast Council has genuine ambiguity.

## Stage A — Neutral decision frame

Write an internal frame that does not favor an answer. Classify the task as one of:

- **Choice:** select among options.
- **Plan review:** determine whether a proposed plan is robust.
- **Idea critique:** find strengths, weaknesses, and conditions for viability.
- **Prioritization:** decide what deserves resources first.
- **Commitment:** decide whether to make a costly or hard-to-reverse move.

If the provided options are a false dichotomy, the First Principles lens may propose a third path.

## Stage B — Five isolated briefs

Keep the lenses semantically different.

### 1. Adversary / Risk

Ask:
- How does this fail?
- What risk is being underpriced?
- Where is there lock-in, fragility, security/privacy exposure, or irreversible downside?
- What second-order effect appears after the obvious first-order benefit?

Do not oppose the plan merely to sound contrarian.

### 2. First Principles

Ask:
- What outcome is actually wanted?
- Which constraints are physics/law/budget/deadline and which are convention?
- Are these the right options?
- Is there a cheaper, simpler, or more reversible path to the same outcome?

### 3. Opportunity / Leverage

Ask:
- Where is asymmetric upside?
- What creates reusable assets, compounding learning, distribution, optionality, or future bargaining power?
- Can one move unlock several later moves?
- Is the team being too conservative because downside is vivid but upside is not?

### 4. Outside View / Stakeholder

Choose the outside observer that has the most leverage over success.

Ask:
- What do comparable situations usually look like?
- What would a customer/buyer/recruiter/investor/teammate/new user misunderstand or distrust?
- What incentive or social expectation is missing from the creator's view?
- What would make the value obvious or non-obvious to someone without insider context?

### 5. Operator / Experiment

Ask:
- Can this be executed with available time, money, skills, and dependencies?
- What is the critical path and bottleneck?
- Which uncertainty can be tested before full commitment?
- What are the kill criteria or stop-loss conditions?
- What is the smallest credible next step?

## Stage C — Optional specialist

Deep mode may add exactly one specialist lens if it materially changes quality. Examples: security, data, finance, GTM, UX, hiring, architecture, compliance.

Do not impersonate a licensed professional. The specialist is a structured analysis perspective using available evidence.

## Stage D — Evidence audit

Use this order of preference:

1. Explicit user facts and constraints.
2. User files and connected sources.
3. Primary/official/current external sources.
4. Strong secondary sources.
5. Estimates and assumptions, clearly labeled.

For decision-critical claims, record whether the claim is verified, assumed, estimated, or unknown.

## Stage E — Blind challenge

Anonymize the briefs conceptually and ask:

- Which claim has the best evidence-to-confidence ratio?
- Which brief depends on the shakiest assumption?
- Which downside is irreversible?
- Which upside is genuinely asymmetric rather than wishful?
- What are all briefs taking for granted?
- What observation would settle the largest disagreement?

## Stage F — Synthesis

Do not vote. Decide using:

- objective fit;
- evidence quality;
- downside severity;
- reversibility;
- option value;
- execution feasibility;
- timing;
- what can be learned cheaply before committing.

### Dominant-constraint rule

If an authoritative hard constraint makes an option unavailable or clearly unacceptable, state that directly and stop pretending the remaining options are equally viable.

### Experiment-before-commitment rule

When evidence is weak and a cheap reversible experiment can answer the key question, recommend the experiment rather than a premature final commitment.

### Anti-confirmation-bias rule

A user preference is context, not proof. Construct the strongest evidence-based countercase to the preferred option before synthesis.
